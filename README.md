# Awesome Scientific Literature Retrieval

[![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

A curated list of papers on retrieval and recommendation for scientific literature — **citation recommendation** (local and global), ad-hoc academic search, agentic literature search, citation-informed document embeddings, reranking, and the benchmarks these systems are evaluated on.

Papers are classified by the **purpose of the retrieval** — what counts as success, and where the gold standard comes from — not by the shape of the query. A system whose query is a user's reading history and one whose query is a draft manuscript belong together if both are judged against the same thing.

## Local Citation Recommendation

The query is a citation context or placeholder inside a manuscript, and the target is **the one paper the author actually cited there**. Success is precision on a single answer, which is why this branch reports Recall@k and MRR against a single gold citation.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) Public Profile Matters: A Scalable Integrated Approach to Recommend Citations in the Wild</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17361">link</a>]</summary> Profiler, a lightweight non-learned module that captures human citation patterns to strengthen candidate retrieval, paired with the DAVINCI reranker; also argues that the usual transductive evaluation is unrealistic and introduces an inductive setting with strict temporal cutoffs that simulates recommending citations for newly written papers.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WASP@AACL'26</i>) Local Citation Recommendation Without Graph Signals</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15832">link</a>]</summary> Releases LCRC, 14,735 statements from astrophysics review papers whose cited targets are available in full text, and builds a recommender that deliberately avoids citation-graph signals, and their pull toward highly cited or recent papers, while beating dense retrieval baselines without fine-tuning.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>COLM'25</i>) ScholarCopilot: Training Large Language Models for Academic Writing with Accurate Citations</b> [<a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=r8nloXtluk">link</a>]</summary> Jointly generates academic prose and retrieves citations by switching from generation to retrieval at learned citation tokens, using the token hidden state as the paper query.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'25</i>) CiteBART: Learning to Generate Citations for Local Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.89">link</a>]</summary> Citation-specific pre-training in an encoder-decoder, where author-date citation tokens are masked and reconstructed so that recommendation becomes generation; contributes a taxonomy of hallucinations with a 4% macro hallucination rate at top-3.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LREC-COLING'24</i>) ILCiteR: Evidence-grounded Interpretable Local Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.63317/4j2msq9yo8o6">link</a>]</summary> Reformulates local citation recommendation so the target space is evidence spans rather than papers, returning ranked evidence-paper pairs from distant supervision with no model training at all.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL Findings'24</i>) SymTax: Symbiotic Relationship and Taxonomy Fusion for Effective Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.533">link</a>]</summary> A three-stage prefetcher-enricher-reranker architecture that embeds query and candidate taxonomies in hyperbolic space, released with ArSyTa, a dataset of 8.27M citation contexts.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ECIR'22</i>) Local Citation Recommendation with Hierarchical-Attention Text Encoder and SciBERT-Based Reranking</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-99736-6_19">link</a>]</summary> Attacks the prefetch stage rather than the reranker, showing a hierarchical-attention encoder beats BM25 at prefetching so a SciBERT reranker needs fewer candidates for the same accuracy.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>Scientometrics'20</i>) A Context-Aware Citation Recommendation Model with BERT and Graph Convolutional Networks</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-020-03561-y">link</a>]</summary> Pairs a GCN document encoder with a BERT context encoder and releases FullTextPeerRead, the first well-organized dataset carrying the context sentences around each citation.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>SIGIR'17</i>) Neural Citation Network for Context-Aware Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3077136.3080730">link</a>]</summary> Frames context-aware citation recommendation as machine translation, encoding the citation context with a max time-delay network augmented by attention and author networks.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WSDM'11</i>) Citation Recommendation without Author Supervision</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/1935826.1935926">link</a>]</summary> Removes the requirement that authors supply a bibliography or mark where citations go: given a bare manuscript, the system finds the passages that need a citation and recommends papers for each by matching manuscript segments against representative segments drawn from a noisy CiteSeerX corpus.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WWW'10</i>) Context-Aware Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/1772690.1772734">link</a>]</summary> Defines the task this section is built on: given a placeholder in a manuscript, recommend papers that fit both the paper as a whole and the local context of that citation place, scored by a non-parametric probabilistic model of context-to-document relevance and prototyped in CiteSeerX.</details>

## Global Citation Recommendation

The query is the gist of a document, a draft idea, or a reader's interests, and the target is **the author's whole reference list**. Success is coverage over a set, reported as MAP and nDCG. Query shape varies widely here -- a manuscript, a seed paper, a research problem or survey topic, an interaction history, an example document plus a facet -- but the purpose does not.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>KAIS'26</i>) SPECTER-BS: Effective Citation Recommendation Using SPECTER with Bibliographic Scoring</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s10115-025-02677-y">link</a>]</summary> A two-stage model pairing SPECTER-based semantic prefetching with a bibliographic score computed from author networks and recent citation counts, targeting the low precision and overfitting of prior models.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WWW'26</i>) What Should I Cite? A RAG Benchmark for Academic Citation Prediction</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3774904.3792075">link</a>]</summary> The first RAG-integrated benchmark for academic citation prediction, with a three-level 554k-paper corpus and an evaluation covering hallucination and diversity alongside retrieval accuracy.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>SIGIR'26</i>) Aspect-Aware Content-Based Recommendations for Mathematical Research Papers</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3805712.3809531">link</a>]</summary> An expert study establishes that relevance between mathematical papers is aspect-driven rather than similarity-driven; AchGNN is an aspect-conditioned heterogeneous GNN over text, citations and author lineage, released with the GoldRiM and SilverRiM datasets.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>SDM'26</i>) MasterSet: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Must-Cite Citation Recommendation in the AI/ML Literature</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17680">link</a>]</summary> Targets must-cite rather than merely relevant citations, labelling each citation by experimental-baseline status, 1–5 core relevance and intra-paper mention frequency with an LLM judge validated on a human-checked sample, over a 150k-paper candidate pool from 15 AI/ML venues.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'26</i>) AgentExpt: Automating AI Experiment Design with LLM-based Resource Retrieval Agent</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/li26cb.html">link</a>]</summary> Recommends the baselines and datasets a new experiment should use, from a knowledge base linking 108,825 accepted papers to the resources they used; each resource is represented by its own description plus the citation contexts others wrote about it, and an LLM reranker reasons over chains of baseline-dataset co-usage.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'26</i>) Berry-LR: Literature Retrieval for Automatic Scientific Survey Generation via Berrypicking Model</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.2678">link</a>]</summary> Collects the reference list for an automatically generated survey as a berrypicking process -- broadening the search move by move and using confident hits as anchors for the next round -- instead of one-shot retrieval from the topic, then selects references using the recorded search trajectory; evaluated on SurGE and SurveyBench.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'26</i>) RSA-CR: Resisting Shilling Attacks in Citation Recommendation via Dumbbell Inductive Learning</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i42.40854">link</a>]</summary> Analyses how shilling attacks such as deliberately inflated citation counts distort citation recommendation, and resists them with a two-layer author-paper graph whose aggregation is weighted by collaboration history, significant citations and content constraints.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'25</i>) Multi-Facet Blending for Faceted Query-by-Example Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.1388">link</a>]</summary> Synthesizes facet-specific training sets by decomposing and recomposing documents, addressing the absence of facet-level relevance labels that forced prior work to fall back on citations as a coarse proxy.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'25</i>) MIR: Methodology Inspiration Retrieval for Scientific Research Problems</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.1390">link</a>]</summary> Defines the task of retrieving prior work whose methods could inspire a solution to a stated research problem, and builds a Methodology Adjacency Graph from citation relations to give dense retrievers a prior beyond semantic similarity, with further gains from LLM reranking.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'25</i>) Scholar Inbox: Personalized Paper Recommendations for Scientists</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08385">link</a>]</summary> An open platform that recommends new papers from a researcher's own ratings, using a map of science and active learning to get past cold start, evaluated on a dataset of 800K user ratings.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'24</i>) HLM-Cite: Hybrid Language Model Workflow for Text-based Scientific Citation Prediction</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-1527">link</a>]</summary> Introduces the notion of core citations and predicts them with a hybrid workflow: a curriculum-finetuned embedding model retrieves from 100K candidates, then LLM agents rerank by one-shot reasoning.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LREC-COLING'24</i>) Recommending Missed Citations Identified by Reviewers: A New Task, Dataset and Baselines</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.63317/33uudfoux4nm">link</a>]</summary> Defines the task of recommending citations that reviewers flagged as missing, contributing the expert-labelled CitationR dataset and an attentive reference encoder over the paper's existing bibliography.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>RecSys'24</i>) GLAMOR: Graph-based LAnguage MOdel Embedding for Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3640457.3688171">link</a>]</summary> Textualizes an attributed knowledge graph through random walks and finetunes a language model on the generated text, targeting the cold-start and name-ambiguity failures of KG-based recommenders.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>TKDE'24</i>) Supporting Your Idea Reasonably: A Knowledge-Aware Topic Reasoning Strategy for Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1109/tkde.2024.3365508">link</a>]</summary> Recommends papers for a rough idea while requiring the recommendation be explainable, extracting multi-hop reasoning paths between knowledge-concept topics from an external knowledge graph.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>KAIS'24</i>) An Academic Recommender System on Large Citation Data Based on Clustering, Graph Modeling and Deep Learning</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s10115-024-02094-7">link</a>]</summary> A multi-stage recommender combining clustering, graph modelling and deep learning that runs on a complete million-scale digital library rather than a sampled subset, arguing that published systems are evaluated at a size unlike real deployments.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>TOIS'22</i>) Graph Neural Collaborative Topic Model for Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3473973">link</a>]</summary> Combines relational topic models with graph neural networks so that indirect, high-order citation links shape both the topics and the recommendations, keeping the reach of a GNN while letting the latent topics explain each recommendation.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>TPDL'21</i>) Citation Recommendation for Research Papers via Knowledge Graphs</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-86324-1_20">link</a>]</summary> Adds a research knowledge graph of scientific concepts on top of SPECTER document embeddings, and evaluates by retrieving from the whole corpus instead of the 30-document candidate sets used by prior work.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'20</i>) Leveraging Title-Abstract Attentive Semantics for Paper Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v34i01.5335">link</a>]</summary> A two-level attentive network that models the semantic relationship between a paper's title and abstract, with the title embedding acting as a memory continuously updated by abstract sentences.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>TOIS'19</i>) Personalised Reranking of Paper Recommendations Using Paper Content and User Behavior</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3312528">link</a>]</summary> The query here is a reader's browsing history rather than a manuscript: reranks the candidates a production academic search engine sends new newsletter subscribers, combining content similarity to previously browsed papers with a model of browse-to-click behaviour.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NAACL'18</i>) Content-Based Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/N18-1022">link</a>]</summary> Citeomatic embeds the query document, takes its nearest neighbours as candidates, then reranks them with a model trained to separate observed from unobserved citations; it deliberately uses no metadata, on the finding that metadata improves the standard metrics while favouring self-citations that are useless as recommendations.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'18</i>) Generative Adversarial Network Based Heterogeneous Bibliographic Network Representation for Personalized Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v32i1.12037">link</a>]</summary> Learns embeddings for papers, authors and venues in one heterogeneous bibliographic network with a generative adversarial network that combines structure and content, and uses the author embeddings to make citation recommendation personalized.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>JIFS'18</i>) Global Citation Recommendation Using Knowledge Graphs</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.3233/jifs-169493">link</a>]</summary> Expands the semantic features of an abstract with DBpedia entities, generates candidates with Lucene MoreLikeThis, and ranks them with LambdaMART over hand-designed pairwise features.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>CIKM'12</i>) Recommending Citations: Translating Papers into References</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/2396761.2398542">link</a>]</summary> Treats the words around citations and the cited papers as parallel text in two languages, with each cited paper a single word, and learns a translation model between them, so a query anywhere from a sentence to a whole manuscript is translated into a ranked reference list.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>SIGIR'07</i>) Recommending Citations for Academic Papers</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/1277741.1277868">link</a>]</summary> Uses an unpublished manuscript as the query, builds candidates from text similarity plus the papers those candidates cite, and ranks them with citation-graph features, an order-of-magnitude MAP gain over text similarity alone.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>CSCW'02</i>) On the Recommending of Citations for Research Papers</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/587078.587096">link</a>]</summary> Applies collaborative filtering to citation recommendation by treating the citation web as the ratings matrix, comparing six algorithms offline on 186K ResearchIndex papers and online with over 120 users.</details>

## Ad-hoc Academic Search

The query is a search string that **underspecifies the intent**, so the system must first work out what is being asked; the gold standard is a human relevance judgement rather than a reference list. Every paper here works to close that gap -- on the query side through concept identification, view recognition, pseudo-query reconstruction, Boolean query formulation or multi-turn clarification, or on the document side by indexing a paper under the words others use when citing it.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'26</i>) PaperRegister: Boosting Flexible-grained Paper Search via Hierarchical Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.798">link</a>]</summary> Transforms an abstract-based index into a hierarchical index tree so queries at any granularity can be served, with a 0.6B view recognizer trained by SFT and GRPO selecting the right level.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) Multi-Turn Agentic Scientific Literature Search via Workflow Induction</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00597">link</a>]</summary> Frames scientific search as workflow induction, constructing an executable DAG of search operators that user feedback refines alongside the query itself, rather than treating feedback as extra query text.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WWW'26</i>) PairSem: LLM-Guided Pairwise Semantic Matching for Scientific Document Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09897">link</a>]</summary> Represents the semantics of queries and papers as LLM-extracted entity-aspect pairs rather than as independent entities, a training-free plug-in that sharpens concept-level matching across base retrievers.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP Findings'25</i>) Scientific Paper Retrieval with LLM-Guided Semantic-Based Ranking</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.findings-emnlp.108">link</a>]</summary> Grounds LLM query understanding in a corpus-derived scientific concept index, making the concepts a query asks about an explicit and controllable matching signal instead of a holistic embedding.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>KDD'25</i>) LitFM: A Retrieval Augmented Structure-aware Foundation Model for Citation Graphs</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3711896.3737028">link</a>]</summary> A literature foundation model whose graph retriever reconstructs pseudo-queries for incomplete or ambiguous input and reranks by a diversity metric to counter the Matthew effect.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WSDM'25</i>) Improving Scientific Document Retrieval with Concept Coverage-based Query Set Generation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3701551.3703544">link</a>]</summary> CCQGen generates synthetic training queries one at a time, conditioning each on the document concepts the previous queries left uncovered, so the query set covers a paper's academic concepts instead of repeating the same few terms.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'24</i>) Taxonomy-guided Semantic Indexing for Academic Paper Search</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.407">link</a>]</summary> A plug-and-play taxonomy-guided semantic index that lets existing dense retrievers match the academic concepts underlying a query, effective even with highly limited training data.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>SIGIR'23</i>) Can ChatGPT Write a Good Boolean Query for Systematic Review Literature Search?</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3539618.3591703">link</a>]</summary> Tests ChatGPT at formulating and refining systematic-review Boolean queries, finding high precision at the cost of recall -- a reasonable trade for rapid reviews -- along with caveats including large variation between runs of the same prompt.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WWW'20</i>) Automatic Boolean Query Formulation for Systematic Review Literature Search</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3366423.3380185">link</a>]</summary> Automates what an information specialist does when turning a systematic review protocol into a Boolean query: parse the logical structure of its concepts, map them to UMLS entities for expansion, then post-process the result into a runnable PubMed query.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'20</i>) Keyphrase Generation for Scientific Document Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.105">link</a>]</summary> Expands papers with keyphrases from sequence-to-sequence generators and shows this significantly improves retrieval, using retrieval as an extrinsic evaluation that exposes where generation falls short -- keyphrases absent from the text, and transfer across domains.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WWW'17</i>) Explicit Semantic Ranking for Academic Search via Knowledge Graph Embedding</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3038912.3052558">link</a>]</summary> Traces a major error source in Semantic Scholar's query log to research concepts the engine did not understand, and ranks queries and papers by their connections in a knowledge-graph embedding space, improving the production system most on hard queries.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>CIKM'08</i>) Comparing Citation Contexts for Information Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/1458082.1458113">link</a>]</summary> Works on the document side: indexes a paper under the terms that citing papers use around their citations of it, and compares how much context to take, from fixed windows and linguistically motivated spans up to the whole citing paper.</details>

## Agentic Literature Search

The query is an open research question and the system answers it by **acting** -- issuing a search, reading what comes back, and deciding what to search next. Success is judged on what the agent finally returns -- a synthesized answer with its citations, or a collected set of papers -- so retrieval quality is only visible through that end product, which is why several of these papers ship their own benchmark: no ranked-list metric measures it.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>Nature'26</i>) Synthesizing Scientific Literature with Retrieval-Augmented Language Models</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-10072-4">link</a>]</summary> OpenScholar, a retrieval-augmented LM over 45M open-access papers that answers scientific queries with citation-backed synthesis through a self-feedback inference loop; released with ScholarQABench and reaching citation accuracy on par with human experts where GPT-4o hallucinates citations most of the time.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'26</i>) SciLENS: RL-Driven Autonomous Agents for Scientific Localized Evidence Navigation and Synthesis</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.589">link</a>]</summary> Works entirely over a local copy of a large academic corpus instead of online search, with offline tools for traversing citation networks and visualizing trends; trained without human labels on synthesized multi-hop data checked by cross-model consensus, then rubric-based reinforcement learning.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'26</i>) PubMed Reasoner: Dynamic Reasoning-based Retrieval for Evidence-Grounded Biomedical Question Answering</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.763">link</a>]</summary> Critiques and refines its PubMed query's MeSH terms against a cheap metadata-only retrieval before searching in full, then reads articles in batches until the evidence suffices; reaches 78.32% on PubMedQA with a GPT-4o backbone, slightly above human experts.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EACL'26</i>) SciRAG: Adaptive, Citation-Aware, and Outline-Guided Retrieval and Synthesis for Scientific Literature</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.eacl-long.303">link</a>]</summary> Alternates adaptively between sequential and parallel evidence gathering, uses the citation graph to organize and filter supporting papers, and plans and critiques an outline before synthesizing; evaluated on QASA and ScholarQA.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>COLM'25</i>) DeepRetrieval: Hacking Real Search Engines and Retrievers with Large Language Models via Reinforcement Learning</b> [<a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=u9JXu4L17I">link</a>]</summary> Trains a 3B model by reinforcement learning to write queries against real search engines, rewarded by retrieval metrics alone, which removes the labelled query-augmentation pairs that supervised query rewriting depends on.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'25</i>) PaSa: An LLM Agent for Comprehensive Academic Paper Search</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.572">link</a>]</summary> Decides for itself when to call search tools, read papers and follow their references, trained by reinforcement learning on 35K synthetic queries from AI conference papers; on RealScholarQuery, a benchmark of real academic queries, PaSa-7B beats Google with GPT-4o by about 38% in recall@20.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'24</i>) Language Agents Achieve Superhuman Synthesis of Scientific Knowledge</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13740">link</a>]</summary> PaperQA2, an agentic RAG system for literature research evaluated against subject-matter experts given unrestricted time and tools; contributes the LitQA2 benchmark and applies retrieval to contradiction detection across papers.</details>

## Embedding & Dense Retrieval

The first stage of the pipeline: scientific document embeddings and first-stage retrievers, and the benchmarks that evaluate them. What mostly separates them is the supervision signal -- citation links, co-citation contexts, search clicks, journal or MeSH labels, or LLM-synthesized training data. These papers define no retrieval task of their own -- an embedding plus cosine similarity is already a working retriever, and several systems in the sections above use exactly that with no second stage. Also here is the capacity result that bounds all of them: the embedding dimension limits which sets of top-k results a single vector can return at all.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'26</i>) REPAIR: Resolving Long-Tail Confusion in Scientific Retrievers via Fact-Verified Iterative Refinement</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.1204">link</a>]</summary> Self-evolving data augmentation for scientific dense retrievers: diagnoses which long-tail concepts the retriever confuses, expands evidence for them through external APIs, and mines hard negatives to tell them apart, beating 19 baselines on nine materials-science and biomedical benchmarks.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'26</i>) SemCSE-Multi: Multifaceted and Decodable Embeddings for Aspect-Specific and Interpretable Scientific Domain Mapping</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1884">link</a>]</summary> Produces multiple individually specifiable aspect embeddings per abstract in a single forward pass, and decodes embeddings back into natural-language descriptions of the aspect they capture.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'26</i>) BioHiCL: Hierarchical Multi-Label Contrastive Learning for Biomedical Retrieval with MeSH Labels</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-short.62">link</a>]</summary> Uses the MeSH hierarchy as graded multi-label supervision for contrastive learning instead of binary relevance, so two biomedical texts can be partly related; compact 0.1B and 0.3B models for retrieval, sentence similarity and question answering.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) UniFAR: A Unified Facet-Aware Retrieval Framework for Scientific Documents</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23766">link</a>]</summary> Serves both paper-to-paper and question-to-paper retrieval from one representation space, with multi-granularity encoding that reconciles short questions with long documents and learnable facet anchors that model which semantic role a query targets.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'26</i>) BiCA: Effective Biomedical Dense Retrieval with Citation-Aware Hard Negatives</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i39.40583">link</a>]</summary> Mines hard negatives from multi-hop citation chains in PubMed, showing that document link structure yields highly informative negatives and reaches state-of-the-art retrieval with minimal finetuning.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'26</i>) On the Theoretical Limitations of Embedding-Based Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=k9CzIvzfaA">link</a>]</summary> Connects known learning-theory results to retrieval: the number of top-k document subsets an embedding model can return is bounded by the embedding dimension, and this holds even when the vectors are optimized directly on the test set; the LIMIT dataset then breaks state-of-the-art models with trivially simple queries.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'25</i>) SemCSE: Semantic Contrastive Sentence Embeddings Using LLM-Generated Summaries for Scientific Abstracts</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.1662">link</a>]</summary> Learns scientific text embeddings from LLM-generated summaries rather than citation links, on the argument that citation-based approaches do not necessarily reflect semantic similarity.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'25</i>) CASPER: Concept-integrated Sparse Representation for Scientific Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13394">link</a>]</summary> A sparse retriever whose dimensions include keyphrases as well as tokens, so queries and papers match on research concepts; trained on how concepts are expressed across scholarly references -- titles, citation contexts, author-assigned keyphrases and co-citations.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'24</i>) MixGR: Enhancing Retriever Generalization for Scientific Domain through Complementary Granularity</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.579">link</a>]</summary> Zero-shot fusion of query-document similarity computed at several granularities of both query and document, lifting unsupervised, supervised and LLM-based retrievers on scientific queries made of multiple sub-queries.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>Bioinformatics'23</i>) MedCPT: Contrastive Pre-trained Transformers with Large-Scale PubMed Search Logs for Zero-Shot Biomedical Information Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btad651">link</a>]</summary> Trains a retriever and a reranker as a pair on 255M PubMed click logs, making search behaviour the relevance signal — a third option beside citation edges and expert assessors, and one available only to whoever operates the search engine.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>SIGIR'23</i>) LADER: Log-Augmented DEnse Retrieval for Biomedical Literature Search</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3539618.3592005">link</a>]</summary> A plug-in module that adds to a dense retriever's scores the clicked documents of similar past queries, weighted by query similarity; state of the art on TripClick, with the largest gain on frequent queries, where click evidence is densest.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'23</i>) SciRepEval: A Multi-Format Benchmark for Scientific Document Representations</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.338">link</a>]</summary> A 24-task benchmark across four task formats showing that SPECTER and SciNCL fail to generalize across formats, and that learning several format-specific embeddings per document does better.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'23</i>) MIReAD: Simple Method for Learning High-quality Representations from Scientific Documents</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.acl-short.46">link</a>]</summary> Learns paper representations by fine-tuning a transformer to predict the publishing journal from the abstract, over 500K PubMed and arXiv abstracts and 2,000+ journal classes -- the venue as the supervision signal instead of citations.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NAACL'22</i>) Multi-Vector Models with Textual Guidance for Fine-Grained Scientific Document Similarity</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2022.naacl-main.331">link</a>]</summary> ASPIRE represents a paper as several sentence-level aspect vectors instead of one document vector and aligns aspects across papers by optimal transport, supervised by co-citation context sentences — a signal that states how two papers are related, not merely that they are.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'22</i>) Neighborhood Contrastive Learning for Scientific Document Representations with Citation Embeddings</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2022.emnlp-main.802">link</a>]</summary> Replaces SPECTER's discrete citation signal with controlled nearest-neighbour sampling over citation graph embeddings, giving a continuous similarity that avoids positive-negative collisions.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'20</i>) SPECTER: Document-level Representation Learning Using Citation-informed Transformers</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.207">link</a>]</summary> Pretrains a Transformer on the citation graph as a document-level relatedness signal, producing embeddings usable without task-specific finetuning, released with the SciDocs benchmark.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'18</i>) hyperdoc2vec: Distributed Representations of Hypertext Documents</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/P18-1222">link</a>]</summary> Embeds hyper-documents such as academic papers with two vectors each, one for the paper as a citing document and one as a citation target, against four criteria such embeddings should satisfy; evaluated on paper classification and citation recommendation.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'19</i>) SciBERT: A Pretrained Language Model for Scientific Text</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/D19-1371">link</a>]</summary> BERT pretrained from scratch on 1.14M Semantic Scholar papers with its own scientific vocabulary rather than continued pretraining on general text; the gain comes from domain vocabulary rather than architecture, and it is the encoder underneath SPECTER and most scientific rerankers since.</details>

## Reranking

The second stage: reordering a prefetched candidate set for precision at the top. Three of the papers here diagnose the same bottleneck -- an LLM context window cannot hold enough candidates -- and split into two orthogonal answers, compressing the candidates or reranking fewer of them. The other two change what the reranker scores: one relevance score per facet instead of a single similarity, or whether a document's evidence actually settles the claim being checked.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'26</i>) Compress-then-Rank: Faster and Better Listwise Reranking with Large Language Models via Ranking-Aware Passage Compression</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i41.40811">link</a>]</summary> Performs listwise reranking on compact multi-vector surrogates rather than full passages, with the compressor pretrained on restoration and continuation objectives and jointly optimized with the reranker.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>KDD'26</i>) CoRank: LLM-Based Compact Reranking with Document Features for Scientific Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2505.13757">link</a>]</summary> A training-free, model-agnostic framework that reranks coarsely over offline-extracted semantic features before refining on full text, so many more candidates fit inside the context window.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) Beyond Single-Score Ranking: Facet-Aware Reranking for Controllable Diversity in Paper Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16329">link</a>]</summary> SciFACE trains separate Background and Method cross-encoders so the user chooses why two papers should count as related, learning from 5,891 GPT-4o-mini-labelled pairs validated against human judgement; on CSFCube it beats FaBLE on Method with far fewer labels than synthetic augmentation uses.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'25</i>) AcuRank: Uncertainty-Aware Adaptive Computation for Listwise Reranking</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2505.18512">link</a>]</summary> Models document relevance as a Bayesian TrueSkill posterior and reranks only the candidates whose ranking remains uncertain, allocating compute adaptively rather than over a fixed-size subset.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>CIKM'25</i>) +VeriRel: Verification Feedback to Enhance Document Retrieval for Scientific Fact Checking</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3746252.3760822">link</a>]</summary> Folds verification success into document ranking for scientific fact checking, so a document is ranked by whether its evidence settles the claim rather than by topical relevance alone, improving evidence retrieval and downstream verification on SciFact, SciFact-Open and Check-COVID.</details>

## Benchmarks & Test Collections

Evaluation resources treated as objects of study rather than as numbers to quote. What separates them is **where relevance comes from** -- expert assessors, the papers' own authors, rhetorical facets, LLM annotation validated against experts, the citation structure itself, or counterfactual edits that hold relevance fixed -- which is the same question that separates the task sections above.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'26</i>) SciNet: Evaluating AI Agents in Relation-Aware Scientific Literature Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/shao26h.html">link</a>]</summary> 8,940 tasks over a 269M-paper meta-database across seven disciplines that test whether retrieval agents understand how papers relate -- retrieving papers with novel knowledge structures, identifying the relation between a pair, reconstructing a line of scientific evolution; agent accuracy is often below 20%.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'26</i>) AstaBench: Rigorous Benchmarking of AI Agents with a Scientific Research Suite</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21652">link</a>]</summary> 2,400+ problems spanning the research process, literature search and synthesis among them, many drawn from real requests to deployed Asta agents, run in a reproducible environment with production-grade search tools and cost-aware scoring; 57 agents evaluated.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'26</i>) Retrieving Facts or Reinforcing Beliefs? Towards Understanding Confirmation Bias in RAG Systems for Scientific Claim Verification</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.44">link</a>]</summary> CoBiT, 707 minimally perturbed claim-counterclaim pairs for measuring confirmation bias in scientific RAG stage by stage; across OpenScholar, ScholarQA and LLM baselines, bias arises at different stages and is amplified by reranking and generation.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'26</i>) SciRerankBench: Benchmarking Rerankers Towards Scientific Retrieval-Augmented Generation with LLMs</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.2613">link</a>]</summary> Evaluates 13 rerankers inside RAG pipelines across five scientific subjects using three kinds of adversarial context -- noisy, semantically similar but logically irrelevant, and counterfactual -- to isolate the reranker's contribution to the final answer.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'26</i>) Authority Bias in Conversational Search Engines for Academic Paper Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.1069">link</a>]</summary> Holds each paper's title and abstract fixed while flipping or boosting its author prestige, venue and citation count, showing that eight LLMs recommending papers favour authority over content, and that debiasing prompts suppress mentions of authority faster than they change the choices.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) MUSES: A Benchmark for Prospective Intellectual-Roots Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00313">link</a>]</summary> Retrieve the earlier papers that later proved generative for a focal paper, over a fixed 2.33M-paper corpus, with labels from citation rhetoric and from authors' own confirmation of what inspired them, and tiers by how familiar those roots are.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) Inspire: Benchmarking Scientific Literature Search for Open Research Problems</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33233">link</a>]</summary> Agents search an open corpus from a solution-redacted research problem, with a cutoff before the target paper appeared, and are scored against the graded antecedents that paper went on to cite; search logs separate exposure, selection and ranking failures, and the best agent reaches 0.284 nDCG@10.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) Can Retrievers Find the Same Paper from Different Aspects? A Multi-Aspect Full-Paper Scientific Retrieval Benchmark</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15624">link</a>]</summary> MAPLE, 2,095 expert-validated queries about recent ML and NLP papers, each aimed at a paper's motivation, method or findings, testing whether a retriever recovers the same paper from every aspect rather than scoring query-paper pairs in isolation.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) RATIO: A Benchmark for Retrieval Across Typed Ideation Operations in Scientific Literature</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27394">link</a>]</summary> Defines relevance by ideation move -- Address retrieves approaches to a stated problem, Broaden more general formulations, Specify concrete instantiations -- built from millions of full-text CS papers by extending discourse-marker distant supervision to corpus-scale retrieval, with LLM and human vetting.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) SciToT: Multimodal Tip-of-the-Tongue Retrieval for Scientific Papers</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05829">link</a>]</summary> Re-finding a half-remembered paper: 11,499 LLM-generated queries that mimic partial recollections, including memories of figures and plots, over 108K open-access papers in five domains, with the cues modelled on real re-finding requests.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) AutoResearchBench: Benchmarking AI Agents on Complex Scientific Literature Discovery</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25256">link</a>]</summary> Agentic literature discovery in two modes: Deep Research tracks down one specific paper through multi-step probing, and Wide Research collects every paper that meets a set of conditions when the number that qualify is unknown.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) SAGE: Benchmarking and Improving Retrieval for Deep Research Agents</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05975">link</a>]</summary> 1,200 scientific literature search questions across four domains over a 200K-paper corpus, with open-ended questions graded against the shared references of paper pairs; BM25 beats LLM-based retrievers by about 30% inside deep research agents because the agents issue keyword-style sub-queries.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'25</i>) BRIGHT: A Realistic and Challenging Benchmark for Reasoning-Intensive Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=ykuc5q381b">link</a>]</summary> 1,398 real-world queries across economics, psychology, mathematics, coding and other domains where surface matching does not suffice; the MTEB leader drops to 18.0 nDCG@10, and reasoning explicitly about the query recovers up to 12.2 points.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'24</i>) LitSearch: A Retrieval Benchmark for Scientific Literature Search</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.840">link</a>]</summary> 597 literature search questions about recent ML and NLP papers, half derived from inline-citation paragraphs and half written by the papers' own authors, all expert-checked; commercial search engines trail the best dense retriever by 32 points.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS D&B'23</i>) DORIS-MAE: Scientific Document Retrieval Using Multi-Level Aspect-Based Queries</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/78f9c04bdcb06f1ada3902912d8b64ba-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html">link</a>]</summary> One hundred human-authored complex research queries, each decomposed into a hierarchy of aspects and sub-aspects with graded relevance, expandable to 4,000+ sub-query cases without further annotation; annotation is by LLM validated against experts at 500x lower cost.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS D&B'21</i>) CSFCube: A Test Collection of Computer Science Research Articles for Faceted Query by Example</b> [<a href="https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/20f07591c6fcb220ffe637cda29bb3f6-Abstract-round2.html">link</a>]</summary> Poses faceted query-by-example for scientific papers -- the query is a paper plus a chosen rhetorical facet, background or method or result -- with fifty expert-annotated query documents and graded relevance following TREC depth-k pooling.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'20</i>) Fact or Fiction: Verifying Scientific Claims</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.emnlp-main.609">link</a>]</summary> Introduces scientific claim verification -- retrieve abstracts that support or refute a claim and identify the rationale sentences -- over SciFact, 1.4K expert-written claims with sentence-level annotation, making the retrieval target an evidence span rather than a document.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>SIGIR Forum'20</i>) TREC-COVID: Constructing a Pandemic Information Retrieval Test Collection</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3451964.3451965">link</a>]</summary> A TREC-style test collection built over CORD-19 while the pandemic was ongoing, with topics evolving round by round as the literature exploded and relevance judged by biomedical experts through depth-k pooling.</details>

## Contributing

If you think a paper is missing or miscategorized, please open an issue or submit a pull request.

Entries use a collapsible block, so the summary stays hidden until the title is clicked:

```html
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>Venue'YY</i>) Paper title</b> [<a href="url">link</a>]</summary> One-line summary.</details>
```

The summary line is raw HTML, so use `<b>`/`<i>`/`<a>` rather than Markdown syntax inside it.

Link text is `link`, not `pdf` — each URL points at the paper's official page of record (DOI, publisher, or proceedings), which is not always a direct PDF. Where a paper is accepted but its proceedings are not yet indexed, the venue label names the accepting venue and the link goes to arXiv.

Venue labels name the venue of record: `ACL Findings` and `EMNLP Findings` are marked as such rather than folded into the main conference, `NeurIPS D&B` denotes the NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks track, `KAIS` denotes the *Knowledge and Information Systems* journal, `JIFS` the *Journal of Intelligent & Fuzzy Systems*, and a workshop paper is labelled with its host conference, as in `WASP@AACL`. Where a paper is better known by a name that is not its published title — Citeomatic, OpenScholar, PaperQA2, SciFact — the title is given as published and the name appears in the summary.

One-line summaries closely follow each paper's stated contribution; consult the original paper for the authors' complete claims and context.
