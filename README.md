# Awesome Scientific Literature Retrieval

[![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

A curated list of papers on retrieval and recommendation for scientific literature — agentic literature search, ad-hoc academic search, citation recommendation (global and local), citation-informed document embeddings, reranking, and the benchmarks these systems are evaluated on.

The task sections classify papers by the **purpose of the retrieval** — what counts as success, and where the gold standard comes from — not by the shape of the query. A system whose query is a user's reading history and one whose query is a draft manuscript belong together if both are judged against the same thing.

Three sections are defined instead by **how** a system searches, whatever its task: [Agentic Literature Search](#agentic-literature-search) comes first, and the two pipeline stages the tasks share, [Embedding & Dense Retrieval](#embedding--dense-retrieval) and [Reranking](#reranking), come last. In between, the task sections run from the broadest query to the narrowest -- a search string, a whole manuscript or idea, a single citation slot. A paper that fits both a task section and a method section is listed once, where its contribution lies (the search control, the retriever or reranker, or the task formulation), and the other section points to it. The benchmark subsections follow the same order.

The task sections list **methods**. A paper whose main contribution is a dataset, benchmark or test collection is listed under [Benchmarks & Test Collections](#benchmarks--test-collections), whichever task it evaluates and even when it also reports baselines. Papers from outside scientific literature, or without a search method of their own, that bear on a section's central design question are linked in that section's introduction as context rather than listed.

## Agentic Literature Search

A model **steers the search at run time**: it looks at what has come back and decides what to do next -- which query to issue or tool to call, which paper to expand, whether to keep going. That run-time decision is the test for this section, whatever the paper calls itself. A pipeline of role-prompted LLMs whose rounds, expansions and stopping are fixed in advance is not agentic here even when its authors call it multi-agent, so iterative pipelines such as Chain of Retrieval and Berry-LR are listed under [Global Citation Recommendation](#global-citation-recommendation), and one-shot query rewriting such as DeepRetrieval under [Ad-hoc Academic Search](#ad-hoc-academic-search). Success is usually judged on what the agent finally returns -- a synthesized answer with its citations, or a collected set of papers -- which is why several of these papers ship their own benchmark.

The agents split on how the search policy is obtained: SPAR, PaperQA2, PubMed Reasoner and SciRAG prompt a frozen LLM, PaSa and PaperScout train a small model with reinforcement learning, and PaperPilot distills one from a teacher's workflows. Whether that training pays off is open: [GEPA](https://openreview.net/forum?id=RQm2KQTM5r) (ICLR'26) finds reflective prompt optimization beats GRPO with far fewer rollouts, and [Yue et al.](https://openreview.net/forum?id=4OsgYD7em5) (NeurIPS'25) find that RL with verifiable rewards mostly sharpens reasoning the base model already has. A third option trains the retriever the agent calls, on the agent's own sub-queries: [LRAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04949) (SIGIR'26) keeps the policy frozen, while [Agentic-R](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.785/) (ACL Findings'26) alternates retriever training with PPO training of the policy, both for open-domain question answering.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'26</i>) PubMed Reasoner: Dynamic Reasoning-based Retrieval for Evidence-Grounded Biomedical Question Answering</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.763">link</a>]</summary> Treats PubMed's search engine as a black box: an LLM writes a Boolean query of MeSH terms (AND between core concepts, OR between synonyms), submits it, reads the titles and abstracts that come back, and adds, drops or regroups terms until the results stop improving; it then reads the hits in rank order, a batch at a time, and stops as soon as the evidence collected answers the question.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EACL'26</i>) SciRAG: Adaptive, Citation-Aware, and Outline-Guided Retrieval and Synthesis for Scientific Literature</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.eacl-long.303">link</a>]</summary> Answers a research question by drafting, critiquing and retrieving in a loop: an LLM drafts an outline, finds what the draft is missing, and issues new sub-queries either one after another to go deeper or side by side to cover more aspects, until it decides to stop. Papers found are expanded along forward and backward citations and arranged by an LLM into chains of theory, method, experiment and application, and those chains, not embedding similarity, decide which papers are kept as evidence.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) PaperScout: An Autonomous Agent for Academic Paper Search with Process-Aware Sequence-Level Policy Optimization</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10029">link</a>]</summary> At each step a 4B model looks at the papers found so far, split into those whose references it has already followed and those it has not, and decides whether to run a new keyword search (Search) or follow a paper's references (Expand). It is trained by reinforcement learning with PSPO, which credits each whole response as one action instead of spreading a step's reward over its tokens.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) Multi-Turn Agentic Scientific Literature Search via Workflow Induction</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00597">link</a>]</summary> PaperPilot: given an anchor paper and a request such as "find follow-up work", the agent assembles a search workflow from typed operators -- keyword search, citation expansion, filters, scoring, LLM reranking -- and runs it, then asks the user a clarifying question and edits the workflow (adding a year filter, changing a weight) instead of rewriting the query text. A 9B model learns this from a teacher's successful workflows and from preference pairs against deliberately broken ones.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'25</i>) PaSa: An LLM Agent for Comprehensive Academic Paper Search</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.572">link</a>]</summary> Two LLM agents share a paper queue: a Crawler that, for the paper at the head of the queue, chooses to issue a new search, add the references cited in one of that paper's sections, or move on, and a Selector that reads each queued paper and keeps or drops it. The 7B Crawler is trained by reinforcement learning on 35K synthetic queries whose answers are the papers cited together in related-work passages of AI conference papers.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'25</i>) SPAR: Scholar Paper Retrieval with LLM-based Agents for Enhanced Academic Search</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15245">link</a>]</summary> A training-free loop of prompted LLM agents: one rewrites the query and picks which sources to search (Google, arXiv, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, PubMed), one judges each result's relevance, the references of the papers it accepts are fetched and judged in turn (one level deep, never recursively), and one writes three new queries from the best papers' methods, applications and limitations for the next round.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'24</i>) Language Agents Achieve Superhuman Synthesis of Scientific Knowledge</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13740">link</a>]</summary> PaperQA2 gives an LLM agent four tools -- keyword paper search, evidence gathering (dense top-k over text chunks, then an LLM summarizes and scores each chunk against the question), citation traversal, and answer generation -- so it can search again or follow citations after seeing how much relevant evidence it has; the authors compare it against domain experts who have unlimited time and internet access, and also use it to find contradictions between papers.</details>

## Ad-hoc Academic Search

The query is a search string that **underspecifies the intent**, so the system must first work out what is being asked; the gold standard is a human relevance judgement rather than a reference list. Every paper here works to close that gap -- on the query side through concept identification, view recognition, pseudo-query reconstruction, Boolean query formulation or query rewriting trained by reinforcement learning, or on the document side by indexing a paper under the words others use when citing it. Multi-turn clarification of the intent appears in PaperPilot, listed under [Agentic Literature Search](#agentic-literature-search) because its contribution is the agent's search control.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'26</i>) PaperRegister: Boosting Flexible-grained Paper Search via Hierarchical Register Indexing</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.798">link</a>]</summary> Indexes each paper not just by its abstract but by an LLM-built tree of its content: fine-grained details such as module configuration or training procedure are extracted from the full text and summarized upward into coarser nodes such as method implementation, with one index per node. At query time a 0.6B view recognizer, trained with SFT and then GRPO, predicts which nodes the query is about, and a paper scores by its best match across those indexes.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WWW'26</i>) PairSem: LLM-Guided Pairwise Semantic Matching for Scientific Document Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09897">link</a>]</summary> An LLM rewrites each paper offline, and each query at search time, as entity-aspect pairs (a compound and its melting point, a model and its post-training) drawn from a corpus-wide vocabulary with synonyms merged, so a match needs the same property of the same thing rather than just the same entities; the pair-level and entity-level similarities are added to any base retriever's score, without training labels.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>COLM'25</i>) DeepRetrieval: Hacking Real Search Engines and Retrievers with Large Language Models via Reinforcement Learning</b> [<a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=u9JXu4L17I">link</a>]</summary> A 3B model rewrites a request into a query for a real search engine such as PubMed or ClinicalTrials.gov, trained by reinforcement learning with the engine's recall as the reward, so no reference queries are needed. It writes the query once without seeing any results, which is why it sits here rather than under agentic search.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP Findings'25</i>) Scientific Paper Retrieval with LLM-Guided Semantic-Based Ranking</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.findings-emnlp.108">link</a>]</summary> SemRank indexes each paper offline by research topics (predicted by a classifier over a topic taxonomy, then pruned by an LLM) and key phrases. At query time it collects the topics and phrases that recur among a base retriever's top papers, has an LLM pick the ones the query is actually about, and reranks papers by those concepts on top of the base score.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>KDD'25</i>) LitFM: A Retrieval Augmented Structure-aware Foundation Model for Citation Graphs</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3711896.3737028">link</a>]</summary> A literature LLM fine-tuned on instruction tasks built from a citation graph, fed by a graph retriever that handles incomplete or ambiguous queries by learning to map them to the embedding of the full query they stand for, and that adds topic-guided candidates so that highly cited papers do not crowd out the rest.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WSDM'25</i>) Improving Scientific Document Retrieval with Concept Coverage-based Query Set Generation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3701551.3703544">link</a>]</summary> CCQGen generates synthetic training queries for a retriever one at a time: it identifies a paper's concepts (taxonomy topics and key phrases) and conditions each new query on the concepts the earlier queries left uncovered, so the set covers the whole paper instead of rephrasing the same few terms.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'24</i>) Taxonomy-guided Semantic Indexing for Academic Paper Search</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.407">link</a>]</summary> TaxoIndex indexes each paper under core topics from an academic taxonomy plus the phrases that signal them, then trains a model to predict that index entry from text, so a query is mapped to topics and phrases it never mentions; papers that share them are boosted on top of an existing dense retriever's similarity.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>SIGIR'23</i>) Can ChatGPT Write a Good Boolean Query for Systematic Review Literature Search?</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3539618.3591703">link</a>]</summary> Prompts ChatGPT to write systematic-review Boolean queries from scratch and to refine existing ones, comparing single prompts with multi-step prompts that follow an information specialist's procedure. The queries are precise but miss studies, change from run to run of the same prompt, and suggest MeSH terms poorly.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WWW'20</i>) Automatic Boolean Query Formulation for Systematic Review Literature Search</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3366423.3380185">link</a>]</summary> Automates what an information specialist does when turning a systematic review protocol into a Boolean query: identify the protocol's concepts and how they combine, map them to UMLS entities to find synonyms, and post-process the result into a query that runs on PubMed.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'20</i>) Keyphrase Generation for Scientific Document Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.105">link</a>]</summary> Appends keyphrases generated by sequence-to-sequence models to each paper's indexed text and measures the change in retrieval, using retrieval as the test of whether generated keyphrases are any good. Retrieval improves even when author keywords are present; the weak spots are keyphrases absent from the text and transfer to other domains.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WWW'17</i>) Explicit Semantic Ranking for Academic Search via Knowledge Graph Embedding</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3038912.3052558">link</a>]</summary> Links queries and papers to research-concept entities, embeds those entities with a knowledge graph built from Semantic Scholar's papers, authors, venues and concepts, and ranks papers by how closely query and paper entities match in that space; motivated by a query-log analysis showing that the production engine failed most often on concepts it did not understand.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>CIKM'08</i>) Comparing Citation Contexts for Information Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/1458082.1458113">link</a>]</summary> Indexes a paper under the words other papers use around their citations of it, and compares how much of that context to take, from fixed windows and linguistically motivated spans up to the whole citing paper.</details>

## Global Citation Recommendation

The query is the gist of a document, a draft idea, or a reader's interests, and the target is **the author's whole reference list**. Success is coverage over a set, reported as MAP and nDCG. Query shape varies widely here -- a manuscript, a seed paper, a research problem or survey topic, an interaction history, an example document plus a facet -- but the purpose does not.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>SIGIR'26</i>) Aspect-Aware Content-Based Recommendations for Mathematical Research Papers</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3805712.3809531">link</a>]</summary> An expert study finds that mathematicians judge two papers related by a specific aspect -- a shared proof technique, a generalization, a logical implication -- rather than by overall similarity. AchGNN conditions a paper's representation on the aspect asked for, using a heterogeneous GNN over the paper's text, its citations and its authors' lineage; released with GoldRiM (expert-annotated) and SilverRiM (derived from zbMATH reviews).</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'26</i>) AgentExpt: Automating AI Experiment Design with LLM-based Resource Retrieval Agent</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/li26cb.html">link</a>]</summary> Recommends the baselines and datasets a new experiment should use, from a knowledge base linking accepted papers to the baselines and datasets they used. Each candidate is represented by its own description plus what other papers wrote when citing it, and a fine-tuned LLM reranks the shortlist by reasoning over which baselines have been used with which datasets.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'26</i>) Berry-LR: Literature Retrieval for Automatic Scientific Survey Generation via Berrypicking Model</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.2678">link</a>]</summary> Collects references for an automatically generated survey in rounds: the topic is split into the moves a survey makes, each move is searched, and the most confidently relevant papers become anchors for the next round's queries. A record of which queries, moves and rounds found each candidate then guides the final selection of references.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'26</i>) RSA-CR: Resisting Shilling Attacks in Citation Recommendation via Dumbbell Inductive Learning</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i42.40854">link</a>]</summary> Defends citation recommendation against shilling attacks such as deliberately inflated citations. Paper embeddings are learned on a two-layer graph: an author layer weighted by past collaborations sets how strongly each paper aggregates from its citations, and confidence-guided inductive aggregation lets newly published papers be embedded without retraining.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'25</i>) Multi-Facet Blending for Faceted Query-by-Example Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.1388">link</a>]</summary> FaBle synthesizes facet-level training data for faceted query-by-example: an LLM summarizes each paper's facets separately, writes similar and dissimilar variants of each facet, and recombines them into pseudo-papers that match an anchor on one facet and differ on the others, so neither facet labels nor citations are needed as relevance signals.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'25</i>) MIR: Methodology Inspiration Retrieval for Scientific Research Problems</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.1390">link</a>]</summary> Defines the task of finding earlier papers whose methods could inspire a solution to a stated research problem. Citations labelled by intent (methodology or not) form a graph of methodological lineage; triplets sampled from it fine-tune dense retrievers toward that lineage rather than topical similarity, and an LLM reranker follows.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'25</i>) Chain of Retrieval: Multi-Aspect Iterative Search Expansion and Post-Order Search Aggregation for Full Paper Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10057">link</a>]</summary> Takes a whole paper as the query: DPO-trained LLMs rewrite it into separate motivation, method and experiment queries, each branch promotes its top hit to be the next query paper for a fixed three rounds, and the resulting tree of searches is merged bottom-up with reciprocal rank fusion. The expansion is fixed rather than chosen by a model, so it is listed here and not under agentic search. Released with SciFullBench.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'25</i>) Scholar Inbox: Personalized Paper Recommendations for Scientists</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08385">link</a>]</summary> An open platform that recommends new papers from arXiv, bioRxiv and similar archives from each researcher's own positive and negative ratings over paper embeddings; new users start from areas they choose on a map of science, and an active-learning loop asks them to rate papers chosen to learn their preferences quickly.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'24</i>) HLM-Cite: Hybrid Language Model Workflow for Text-based Scientific Citation Prediction</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-1527">link</a>]</summary> Separates a paper's core citations -- references that the papers citing it also go on to cite -- from superficial ones, and predicts them in two stages: an embedding model fine-tuned with a curriculum shortlists from up to 100K candidates, then LLM agents reason over the shortlist to rank it.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LREC-COLING'24</i>) Recommending Missed Citations Identified by Reviewers: A New Task, Dataset and Baselines</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.63317/33uudfoux4nm">link</a>]</summary> Defines the task of recommending the citations that reviewers said a submission was missing, with the expert-labelled CitationR dataset; the baseline, RMCNet, encodes a submission together with its existing reference list through an attentive reference encoder, so recommendations account for what the authors already cite.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>RecSys'24</i>) GLAMOR: Graph-based LAnguage MOdel Embedding for Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3640457.3688171">link</a>]</summary> Turns an attributed knowledge graph of papers, authors and venues into text by random walks and fine-tunes a language model on that text, so that new papers without links and authors with ambiguous names can still be embedded and recommended.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>TKDE'24</i>) Supporting Your Idea Reasonably: A Knowledge-Aware Topic Reasoning Strategy for Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1109/tkde.2024.3365508">link</a>]</summary> Recommends papers for a rough research idea and explains each one with a multi-hop path between the idea's knowledge-concept topics and the paper's in an external knowledge graph.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>KAIS'24</i>) An Academic Recommender System on Large Citation Data Based on Clustering, Graph Modeling and Deep Learning</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s10115-024-02094-7">link</a>]</summary> A multi-stage recommender that clusters papers, models the citation graph and ranks with deep learning, run on a complete million-scale digital library rather than a sampled subset, on the argument that published systems are tested at a size unlike real deployments.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>TOIS'22</i>) Graph Neural Collaborative Topic Model for Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3473973">link</a>]</summary> Combines relational topic models with graph neural networks so that indirect, multi-hop citation links shape both the topics and the recommendations, keeping the reach of a GNN while the latent topics explain each recommendation.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>TPDL'21</i>) Citation Recommendation for Research Papers via Knowledge Graphs</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-86324-1_20">link</a>]</summary> Adds a research knowledge graph, which links papers through scientific concepts automatically extracted from their text, to SPECTER document embeddings, and ranks against the whole corpus instead of the 30-document candidate sets used in earlier evaluations.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'20</i>) Leveraging Title-Abstract Attentive Semantics for Paper Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v34i01.5335">link</a>]</summary> Learns a paper's representation from how its title relates to its abstract: each abstract sentence is encoded by an attention LSTM and updates a memory initialised from the title embedding, and a GRU over the papers a user has interacted with models that user's preferences.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>TOIS'19</i>) Personalised Reranking of Paper Recommendations Using Paper Content and User Behavior</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3312528">link</a>]</summary> The query here is a reader's browsing history rather than a manuscript: reranks the candidates a production academic search engine sends to new newsletter subscribers, combining content similarity to the papers they browsed with a model of which browsed papers lead to clicks.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NAACL'18</i>) Content-Based Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/N18-1022">link</a>]</summary> Citeomatic embeds every document's text, takes a query document's nearest neighbours plus the papers those neighbours cite as candidates, and reranks them with a second model trained to separate observed from unobserved citations. It deliberately uses no metadata, which raises the standard metrics mainly by favouring self-citations.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'18</i>) Generative Adversarial Network Based Heterogeneous Bibliographic Network Representation for Personalized Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v32i1.12037">link</a>]</summary> Embeds papers and authors, including the query manuscript and its authors, in one heterogeneous bibliographic network with a generative adversarial network that combines network structure with each object's text, and uses the author embeddings to personalize citation recommendation.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>CIKM'12</i>) Recommending Citations: Translating Papers into References</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/2396761.2398542">link</a>]</summary> Treats the words around citations and the cited papers as parallel text in two languages, with each cited paper a single word, and learns a translation model between them, so a query anywhere from a sentence to a whole manuscript is translated into a ranked reference list.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>SIGIR'07</i>) Recommending Citations for Academic Papers</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/1277741.1277868">link</a>]</summary> Uses an unpublished manuscript as the query, builds candidates from text similarity plus the papers those candidates cite, and ranks them with citation-graph features.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>CSCW'02</i>) On the Recommending of Citations for Research Papers</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/587078.587096">link</a>]</summary> Applies collaborative filtering to citation recommendation by treating the citation web as the ratings matrix -- papers as users, the papers they cite as the items they rated -- and compares six algorithms offline and with real users.</details>

## Local Citation Recommendation

The query is a citation context or placeholder inside a manuscript, and the target is **the one paper the author actually cited there**. Success is precision on a single answer, which is why this branch reports Recall@k and MRR against a single gold citation.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WASP@AACL'26</i>) Local Citation Recommendation Without Graph Signals</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15832">link</a>]</summary> Builds LCRC from astrophysics review papers -- sentences whose citations resolve to papers available in full text -- and assembles a text-only recommender stage by stage: chunked full text works better than LLM-written contribution summaries as the paper representation, the citing sentence is embedded both alone and with the sentences before it and the two rankings are fused, and reranking by citation count or recency hurts, so citation-graph signals are left out.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>COLM'25</i>) ScholarCopilot: Training Large Language Models for Academic Writing with Accurate Citations</b> [<a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=r8nloXtluk">link</a>]</summary> Jointly generates academic prose and retrieves citations: while writing, the model emits a [RET] token where a citation is needed, the token's hidden state serves directly as the query against a paper index, and the retrieved abstracts are fed back into the text being generated; generation and the token's retrieval embedding are trained together.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'25</i>) CiteBART: Learning to Generate Citations for Local Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.89">link</a>]</summary> Turns local citation recommendation into generation: in pre-training, the author-date citation in a citing sentence, such as "(Devlin et al., 2019)", is masked and an encoder-decoder learns to write it back, and the Global variant adds the citing paper's title and abstract to the context; since generated citations can be hallucinated, the paper also classifies the ways they go wrong.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LREC-COLING'24</i>) ILCiteR: Evidence-grounded Interpretable Local Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.63317/4j2msq9yo8o6">link</a>]</summary> Recommends papers for a query by finding passages in existing papers that make a similar statement and cite something there: the papers those passages cite become the recommendations, and the passage is the evidence for why. The ranking comes from distant supervision and pretrained models, with no training.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL Findings'24</i>) SymTax: Symbiotic Relationship and Taxonomy Fusion for Effective Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.533">link</a>]</summary> A prefetcher retrieves candidates from the citation context plus the citing paper's title, abstract and taxonomy categories; an enricher adds papers from the citation network around them; a reranker fuses the citing and candidate papers' taxonomy categories and scores candidates by their separation in hyperbolic space. Released with the ArSyTa dataset.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ECIR'22</i>) Local Citation Recommendation with Hierarchical-Attention Text Encoder and SciBERT-Based Reranking</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-99736-6_19">link</a>]</summary> Targets the prefetch step that BM25 usually fills: a two-level hierarchical-attention encoder embeds paragraphs and then whole documents for nearest-neighbour prefetching, and a fine-tuned SciBERT reranks the shortlist; released with a dataset of local citation sentences from arXiv papers.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>Scientometrics'20</i>) A Context-Aware Citation Recommendation Model with BERT and Graph Convolutional Networks</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-020-03561-y">link</a>]</summary> Encodes the citation context with BERT and each candidate paper with a graph autoencoder over the citation graph alone, without author or venue metadata, and matches the two; released with FullTextPeerRead, which keeps the sentences around each citation.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>SIGIR'17</i>) Neural Citation Network for Context-Aware Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3077136.3080730">link</a>]</summary> Treats context-aware citation recommendation as translation: the encoder represents the citation context with a time-delay network plus attention and adds embeddings of the citing and cited authors, and the decoder generates the cited paper's title.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WSDM'11</i>) Citation Recommendation without Author Supervision</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/1935826.1935926">link</a>]</summary> Removes the requirement that authors supply a bibliography or mark where citations go: given a bare manuscript, the system finds the passages that need a citation and recommends papers for each by matching manuscript segments against representative segments drawn from a noisy CiteSeerX corpus.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>WWW'10</i>) Context-Aware Citation Recommendation</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/1772690.1772734">link</a>]</summary> Defines the task this section is built on: given a placeholder in a manuscript, recommend papers that fit both the paper as a whole and the words around that placeholder, scored by a non-parametric probabilistic model of context-to-document relevance and prototyped in CiteSeerX.</details>

## Embedding & Dense Retrieval

The first stage of the pipeline: scientific document embeddings and first-stage retrievers. What mostly separates them is the supervision signal -- citation links, co-citation contexts, search clicks, journal or MeSH labels, or LLM-synthesized training data. These papers define no retrieval task of their own -- an embedding plus cosine similarity is already a working retriever, and several systems in the sections above use exactly that with no second stage. Also here is the capacity result that bounds all of them: the embedding dimension limits which sets of top-k results a single vector can return at all.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'26</i>) REPAIR: Resolving Long-Tail Confusion in Scientific Retrievers via Fact-Verified Iterative Refinement</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.1204">link</a>]</summary> Trains an LLM-based scientific retriever on data it generates for itself, in rounds: find the long-tail concepts the current retriever confuses, fetch fact-checked documents about them from scientific APIs, and build hard negatives that differ from the right document by a single fact, then retrain.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'26</i>) SemCSE-Multi: Multifaceted and Decodable Embeddings for Aspect-Specific and Interpretable Scientific Domain Mapping</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1884">link</a>]</summary> An LLM writes a separate summary sentence of each abstract for each aspect; an embedding model per aspect learns to place summaries of the same abstract close together, and these models are distilled into one that outputs all aspect embeddings from the abstract in a single pass. A decoder trained on summary-embedding pairs turns any embedding, or a point on a map of the field, back into a sentence describing it.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'26</i>) BioHiCL: Hierarchical Multi-Label Contrastive Learning for Biomedical Retrieval with MeSH Labels</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-short.62">link</a>]</summary> Fine-tunes a general dense retriever with LoRA so that two biomedical texts are as similar as their MeSH labels overlap, counting partial and hierarchical overlap, instead of training on binary relevant/irrelevant pairs; released as compact 0.1B and 0.3B models.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) UniFAR: A Unified Facet-Aware Retrieval Framework for Scientific Documents</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23766">link</a>]</summary> Trains one encoder for both paper-to-paper and question-to-paper retrieval: short questions and long papers are encoded at several granularities and aggregated into the same space, learnable facet anchors let a query match the part of a paper it asks about (background, method, results), and the paper-paper and question-paper contrastive losses are trained together.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'26</i>) BiCA: Effective Biomedical Dense Retrieval with Citation-Aware Hard Negatives</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i39.40583">link</a>]</summary> Mines hard negatives from citations: for a synthetic query generated from a PubMed abstract, it collects the papers that abstract cites and the papers they cite, then walks greedily through the ones most similar to the query to pick negatives that are on topic but are not the source.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'26</i>) On the Theoretical Limitations of Embedding-Based Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=k9CzIvzfaA">link</a>]</summary> Shows that the number of distinct top-k result sets an embedding model can return is bounded by its dimension, so some combinations of relevant documents cannot be retrieved by any query vector, even when the vectors are optimized directly on the test set. The LIMIT dataset turns the bound into trivially simple queries ("Who likes quokkas?") that state-of-the-art embedders fail.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'25</i>) SemCSE: Semantic Contrastive Sentence Embeddings Using LLM-Generated Summaries for Scientific Abstracts</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.1662">link</a>]</summary> Trains scientific text embeddings without citations: an LLM writes several summaries of each abstract, and a contrastive model learns to place summaries of the same abstract together and those of different abstracts apart, on the argument that a citation link does not necessarily mean similar content.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'25</i>) CASPER: Concept-integrated Sparse Representation for Scientific Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13394">link</a>]</summary> A learned sparse retriever whose dimensions include keyphrases as well as vocabulary tokens, so a query and a paper can match on a research concept expressed as a phrase; trained on query-paper pairs mined from scholarly references -- titles, citation contexts, author keyphrases and co-citations.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'24</i>) MixGR: Enhancing Retriever Generalization for Scientific Domain through Complementary Granularity</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.579">link</a>]</summary> Splits queries into sub-queries and documents into propositions, scores every combination of granularities (query or sub-query against document or proposition) with an existing retriever, and fuses the scores, with no training; aimed at scientific queries whose parts match different parts of a document.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>Bioinformatics'23</i>) MedCPT: Contrastive Pre-trained Transformers with Large-Scale PubMed Search Logs for Zero-Shot Biomedical Information Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btad651">link</a>]</summary> Trains a retriever and a reranker as a pair on 255M query-article clicks from PubMed's logs, with the reranker trained on negatives drawn from that retriever so it sees at training time the candidates it will rerank; search clicks are the relevance signal, a source available only to whoever operates the search engine.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>SIGIR'23</i>) LADER: Log-Augmented DEnse Retrieval for Biomedical Literature Search</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3539618.3592005">link</a>]</summary> A plug-in for a dense retriever: for a new query it finds similar past queries in the click logs and adds the documents clicked for them, weighted by query similarity, to the retriever's own scores, with no extra training.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'23</i>) MIReAD: Simple Method for Learning High-quality Representations from Scientific Documents</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.acl-short.46">link</a>]</summary> Fine-tunes SciBERT to predict which journal published a paper from its title and abstract, over PubMed and arXiv papers and 2,000+ journals, and uses the resulting representation as the paper embedding: the venue, not citations, is the supervision.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NAACL'22</i>) Multi-Vector Models with Textual Guidance for Fine-Grained Scientific Document Similarity</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2022.naacl-main.331">link</a>]</summary> ASPIRE represents a paper as one vector per abstract sentence instead of one per document and matches papers by aligning their sentences with optimal transport. Supervision comes from sentences that cite several papers together, which say how those papers are related, not merely that they are.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'22</i>) Neighborhood Contrastive Learning for Scientific Document Representations with Citation Embeddings</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2022.emnlp-main.802">link</a>]</summary> SciNCL replaces SPECTER's citation triplets with samples from a citation-graph embedding: positives are a paper's near neighbours in that embedding and negatives are taken from a band further out, so similar papers that never cite each other are not treated as negatives.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'20</i>) SPECTER: Document-level Representation Learning Using Citation-informed Transformers</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.207">link</a>]</summary> Fine-tunes SciBERT with a triplet loss in which a paper's title and abstract must embed closer to papers it cites than to papers it does not, with papers cited by its citations as hard negatives; at inference only the text is needed, so new, uncited papers can be embedded. Released with the SciDocs benchmark.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ACL'18</i>) hyperdoc2vec: Distributed Representations of Hypertext Documents</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/P18-1222">link</a>]</summary> Embeds each hyper-document, such as an academic paper, with two vectors, one for its role as a citing document and one as a citation target, and states four criteria such embeddings should satisfy; evaluated on paper classification and citation recommendation.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>EMNLP'19</i>) SciBERT: A Pretrained Language Model for Scientific Text</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/D19-1371">link</a>]</summary> BERT pretrained from scratch on 1.14M Semantic Scholar papers with a vocabulary built from that corpus rather than continued from general-domain BERT; it is the encoder underneath SPECTER and most scientific rerankers since.</details>

## Reranking

The second stage: reordering a prefetched candidate set for precision at the top. Three of the papers here diagnose the same bottleneck -- an LLM context window cannot hold enough candidates -- and split into two orthogonal answers, compressing the candidates or reranking fewer of them. The fourth changes what the reranker scores: whether a document's evidence actually settles the claim being checked.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'26</i>) Compress-then-Rank: Faster and Better Listwise Reranking with Large Language Models via Ranking-Aware Passage Compression</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i41.40811">link</a>]</summary> C2R has an LLM listwise reranker read each candidate as a short sequence of precomputed vectors instead of its full text, with an index token such as "[1]" marking where each candidate starts; the compressor that produces the vectors is pretrained to restore and continue passages, then fine-tuned together with the reranker on the ranking objective.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>KDD'26</i>) CoRank: LLM-Based Compact Reranking with Document Features for Scientific Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2505.13757">link</a>]</summary> An LLM extracts compact features from each paper offline -- categories, section outline, pseudo-queries, keywords -- so many more candidates fit in one prompt for a first listwise ranking; only the top of that ranking is then reranked on full text. Training-free and works with any LLM.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'25</i>) AcuRank: Uncertainty-Aware Adaptive Computation for Listwise Reranking</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2505.18512">link</a>]</summary> Keeps a Bayesian (TrueSkill) estimate of each candidate's relevance and its uncertainty, repeatedly sends only the candidates whose place relative to the top-k cutoff is still uncertain to the LLM reranker in small groups, updates the estimates, and stops once the top k is settled, instead of sliding a fixed window over the whole list.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>CIKM'25</i>) +VeriRel: Verification Feedback to Enhance Document Retrieval for Scientific Fact Checking</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3746252.3760822">link</a>]</summary> For scientific fact checking, trains the reranker on a target that combines a document's topical relevance with a verifier's verdict on it, so documents whose evidence supports or refutes the claim rank above ones that are merely on topic.</details>

## Benchmarks & Test Collections

Evaluation resources treated as objects of study rather than as numbers to quote. What separates them is **where relevance comes from** -- expert assessors, the papers' own authors, rhetorical facets, LLM annotation validated against experts, the citation structure itself, or counterfactual edits that hold relevance fixed -- which is the same question that separates the task sections above.

Each subsection opens with a table of the **datasets** it covers, followed by the papers. A benchmark often bundles several datasets built and scored differently, and several method papers ship a dataset of their own (ScholarQABench in OpenScholar, LitQA2 in PaperQA2, RealScholarQuery in PaSa), so the tables list datasets rather than papers. The **Gold** column says who decided the correct answers and what form they take. Benchmarks graded on a reference answer or rubric score the final answer and its citations, not a ranked list, so their numbers do not compare with recall on a test collection. [BENCHMARKS.md](BENCHMARKS.md) gives one row per dataset or subtask, with query source, gold source and form, metrics, and, where the paper reports them, date cutoffs and a headline result.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) ScholarCatalyst: A Benchmark for Retrieving Papers That Inspire New Research</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02202">link</a>]</summary> Asks whether a system can find the prior papers that did or could have advanced a project while it was still taking shape: queries are the project's research questions, the corpus stops before the project began, and relevance is judged by the papers' own lead authors. Agentic search does no better than the embedding retriever it calls.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) MUSES: A Benchmark for Prospective Intellectual-Roots Retrieval</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00313">link</a>]</summary> Retrieve the earlier papers that later proved generative for a focal paper, prospectively over a fixed corpus; labels come from citation contexts classified as generative and from the focal authors' own answers about what inspired them, and the roots are tiered by how familiar they are.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) Inspire: Benchmarking Scientific Literature Search for Open Research Problems</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33233">link</a>]</summary> Agents search arXiv from a research problem with its solution removed, with a date cutoff before the target paper appeared, and are scored against the earlier work that paper went on to cite, graded by an LLM ensemble; the search logs separate never seeing a relevant paper, not selecting it, and ranking it too low.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) RATIO: A Benchmark for Retrieval Across Typed Ideation Operations in Scientific Literature</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27394">link</a>]</summary> Defines relevance by ideation move -- Address retrieves approaches to a stated problem, Broaden more general formulations, Specify concrete instantiations -- with sentence-level gold mined from full-text CS papers through the discourse markers that signal each move, then filtered by an LLM judge and checked by people.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'26</i>) PreScience: A Dataset and Benchmark for Scientific Forecasting</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20459">link</a>]</summary> Seven forecasting tasks over recent AI papers on arXiv. The retrieval task is prior-work selection: given a team of collaborators and their past papers, surface the earlier work most likely to shape their next paper, judged against that paper's influential references; the others predict collaborators, contributions, citation counts and topic trends.</details>

## Contributing

If you think a paper is missing or miscategorized, please open an issue or submit a pull request.

What gets in: papers published at a peer-reviewed venue. An arXiv-only preprint is listed when it comes from a group with an established record on the problem, or when the system or resource is already in wide use; otherwise it waits until it is accepted. Theses and course reports are not listed.

Entries use a collapsible block, so the summary stays hidden until the title is clicked:

```html
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>Venue'YY</i>) Paper title</b> [<a href="url">link</a>]</summary> One-line summary.</details>
```

The summary line is raw HTML, so use `<b>`/`<i>`/`<a>` rather than Markdown syntax inside it.

Link text is `link`, not `pdf` — each URL points at the paper's official page of record (DOI, publisher, or proceedings), which is not always a direct PDF. Where a paper is accepted but its proceedings are not yet indexed, the venue label names the accepting venue and the link goes to arXiv.

Venue labels name the venue of record: `ACL Findings` and `EMNLP Findings` are marked as such rather than folded into the main conference, `NeurIPS D&B` denotes the NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks track, `KAIS` denotes the *Knowledge and Information Systems* journal, `JIFS` the *Journal of Intelligent & Fuzzy Systems*, and a workshop paper is labelled with its host conference, as in `WASP@AACL`. Where a paper is better known by a name that is not its published title — Citeomatic, PaperQA2, SciFact — the title is given as published and the name appears in the summary.

Summaries say how a method works rather than what it claims, written from the paper's method section rather than its abstract: what goes in, what the system does, and -- for agentic search -- what the model sees and decides at each step. For a benchmark, they say what is asked and where the gold answers come from. Scores are left out; consult the original paper for results and the authors' complete claims.
